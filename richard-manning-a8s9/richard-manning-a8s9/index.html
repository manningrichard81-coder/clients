The week is the unit

Five rules. Thirteen daily ticks. Twenty-eight days. You lift, you move, you get uncomfortable, and you send the log at the end of each week.

The four pillars

What the block is built on

Nothing here is complicated. That's the point — complicated plans die on a Wednesday.

Pillar 01

Clean fuel

No alcohol. No pasta, rice or bread. Fast until midday, then protein and whole food at every meal.

Pillar 02

Rhythm

Same wake time, morning light on your face, phone down at 8pm. Sleep is the whole foundation.

Pillar 03

Heat and cold

Sauna once a week, sixty seconds of cold every day. Deliberate discomfort, safely dosed.

Non-negotiables

Five rules

Not preferences. Not targets. Five lines that don't move for the length of the block, so you stop having the same argument with yourself forty times a week.

The rules — all five, every day, for the whole block
No.RuleWhy it's in
Rule 1 No alcohol None. Not a glass with dinner, not a schooner after the game, not "only on weekends".
  • It costs you the deep sleep you're training for, on the exact nights you'd drink.
  • It's the reliable gateway to breaking rule two, usually within the hour.
  • Plan the out before you walk in: soda and lime, zero-alc beer, or buy the round and don't drink it.
Rule 2 No pasta, rice, bread The three staples that quietly make up most of the volume on a plate. Cut them and portion size sorts itself out without you counting a thing.
  • Pasta→zucchini ribbons, roast veg, lentils, or the same sauce over a bigger serve of meat
  • Rice→cauliflower rice, potato, sweet potato, beans, extra greens
  • Bread, wraps, toast→eggs, lettuce cups, a proper cooked breakfast
  • Servo sandwich at lunch→last night's leftovers, packed the night before
Rule 3 Phone down at 8pm No socials after 8pm — YouTube, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, the lot. Calls and texts are fine. Charge it in another room.
  • The scroll is what pushes bedtime back, not the light. Cut the scroll, get the hour back.
  • It kills the late-night kitchen visit that breaks rules one and two.
  • Read, stretch, talk to someone, or go to bed early. All beat the feed.
Rule 4 Same wake time every day Pick a time you can hold on a Sunday, not just a Tuesday. Alarm out of reach. Weekends included — that's the whole point.
  • A fixed wake time is the strongest single lever on sleep quality. Bedtime follows it on its own.
  • Sleeping in on the weekend gives you jet lag without the holiday.
  • It makes the morning light and the training slot automatic instead of negotiable.
Rule 5 Sun on your face, 5 minutes Every day, outside, no sunglasses. Overcast counts — it's still far brighter than indoors. Earlier is better.
  • Daylight through the eyes is what anchors your body clock, and a solid clock is what makes you sleepy at the right end of the day.
  • Most people get a fraction of the light they need; five minutes outside beats an hour by a window.
  • Sun safety still applies past mid-morning — hat and sunscreen on the skin, but keep the sunglasses off for those minutes.

Non-negotiable in the other direction: protein at every meal, veg at two of them, and enough food overall inside your eating window. This is a swap, not a restriction contest. If you're constantly hungry, flat or light-headed, you're under-eating — message me and we'll fix the volume rather than you toughing it out.

When you break one — and you will

The reset

  • Log it. In the journal, same day, one line. What, when, what was going on.
  • Don't pay it back. No skipped meals, no punishment session, no doubling up tomorrow.
  • Next meal is the reset. Not next Monday.
  • Tell me at the check-in. A pattern I can see is a pattern I can coach around.
The daily work

Protocols

Seven of them. Six are daily and take about fifteen minutes all up. One is a weekly booking. Learn them once, then stop thinking about them.

Your protocols — what to do, and what it's for
ProtocolWhat to doWhy it's in
Hot Sauna — once a week One 45 minute booking. Run it as three rounds of 12–15 minutes with a few minutes out to cool between, not one long sit. Half a litre of water before, more after. Book your sauna →
  • Cardiovascular load without impact. Heat pushes your heart rate up much like a steady walk does, and regular sauna use is associated with lower blood pressure over time.
  • Recovery and sleep. Most people report less muscle soreness in the days after, and an easier time getting to sleep that night.
Cold Finish cold — 60 seconds, every day End your shower with a full minute of cold. Chest and back under the water, not just your legs. Slow exhales, longer than the inhale, until the gasp settles. Build up from 30 seconds in week one if you need to.
  • Alertness that lasts. Cold water triggers a sharp noradrenaline response — most people feel switched on for hours afterwards, without caffeine.
  • Practice at staying calm. A controlled minute in something you don't want to do is a rep for every other uncomfortable decision in the day.
Sunlight 10 minutes, early morning Outside, within an hour or so of waking, no sunglasses. Overcast still counts. Walk, have your coffee out there, whatever gets you out the door.
  • Sets the clock. Morning light through the eyes is the main signal your body uses to time sleep, and it's what makes you tired at a sensible hour tonight.
  • Mood and alertness. Morning light exposure is consistently linked with better mood and a sharper start to the day.
Bedtime Creatine, and the phone away 8g creatine monohydrate with a full glass of water, and the phone down from 8pm. Standard maintenance dosing is 3–5g a day, so 8g is on the high side — take it with plenty of fluid and drop back if it upsets your stomach.
  • The best-evidenced supplement there is. Creatine reliably improves strength and power output alongside resistance training, and helps you hold onto lean mass.
  • A wind-down that isn't a screen. The 8pm cut-off is what actually gets you to bed on time — the supplement is just the cue to start.
  • Check first if you have kidney disease or take medication affecting the kidneys.
Wake up Same time, water, light Up at your alarm time, seven days a week. Big glass of water within ten minutes, with a pinch of Celtic sea salt. Then straight outside for your ten minutes of light.
  • Consistency beats duration. A fixed wake time does more for how you feel than an extra hour at the weekend ever will.
  • Rehydrate first. You've gone eight hours without fluid; the pinch of salt replaces some of what you lost overnight. Skip the salt if you've been told to watch your blood pressure or sodium.
Breath work Box breathing — 10 rounds One round is: in through the nose for 4, hold for 4, out through the nose for 4, hold empty for 4. Ten rounds takes about three minutes. Sit upright, shoulders down, count slowly and evenly. If four feels like a stretch, drop to three.
  • Downshifts the nervous system on command. Slow, even breathing at around six breaths a minute slows heart rate and settles the stress response — useful before a session, a hard conversation, or bed.
  • Focus you can call up. The counting gives your attention one job, which is why it works as a reset in the middle of a messy day.
  • Sitting or lying down only. Never in or near water, and stop if you get light-headed.
Squat sit One minute a day Sit at the bottom of a deep squat for a minute — broken into chunks is fine at first. Heels raised on a book or a plate is completely fine. Hold a doorframe for balance if you need it.
  • Buys back range you've lost. A daily minute at end range keeps ankles, knees and hips moving through the positions a desk takes away.
  • Better squats under load. Comfort at the bottom position transfers straight to depth and control when there's a bar involved.
  • Ease off if you get sharp knee pain — reduce depth, raise the heels more, or hold support.

Also on the daily list. Fast until midday — black coffee only until then, no milk, and coffee stops at midday too. Two litres of water across the day, ten push-ups, and the three food rules. The fasting window suits plenty of people and doesn't suit everyone: if you're light-headed, wrecked by 11am, or it's making you strange about food, tell me and we'll shorten it. It comes out entirely if you're pregnant, diabetic, or have any history of disordered eating.

The safety lines on heat and cold. Get out of the sauna straight away if you're dizzy, nauseous or your heart won't settle, and skip it entirely if you're unwell, have had any alcohol, or are pregnant. Never do the cold in an ice bath alone, never after alcohol, and never hold your breath in or near water. If you have a heart condition or arrhythmia, uncontrolled blood pressure, Raynaud's, epilepsy, kidney disease, or you're pregnant, talk to your GP before starting the heat, cold or creatine — the rest of the protocol still runs in the meantime.
